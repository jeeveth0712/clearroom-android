# ClearRoom: demo video scripts

**Target length: 58 seconds.** Use a saved room with real clap and noise readings. The built-in **Load sample room** has made-up readings; if you use it, say so in the narration. The video and requirements document are assignment deliverables alongside the APK and source.

## Before recording

1. Open your saved measured room from the Room tab. Check that Results shows a verdict and Fixes shows predictions. If you want to show the Log timeline, make a short real day log **before** recording; do not spend the video's minute waiting for one.
2. Set the screen recorder's **microphone to off**. If it is on, Android can give ClearRoom silence during a measurement. Record the narration separately and add it afterwards. Keep notifications out of the recording.
3. Use quick cuts between tabs and scrolls. Show real readings and predictions as labelled by the app. Do not present the built-in sample as a measured classroom or claim that M13 field tests have passed.
4. Record in portrait on the phone. Trim the exported file to **59 seconds or less** and check its duration before submitting.

## Shot list and narration

| Time | Show on the phone | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 s | Room tab: saved room name, size, and 3D classroom. | “This is ClearRoom. It checks whether students can understand speech at each seat using one Android phone.” |
| 8–18 s | Measure tab: seat picker, saved clap echo times, noise and voice sections. A brief live level meter shot is optional. | “I describe the room, then its microphone measures a clap, background noise, and the speaker’s voice.” |
| 18–32 s | Results: back-row verdict and range, main cause, 3D classroom; switch briefly to Seat map. | “ClearRoom combines those readings with distance to estimate clarity at every seat and explain the main problem. The room view shows where speech is hardest to follow.” |
| 32–44 s | Fixes: ranked changes; move one curtain slider and show the predicted before and after. | “I can try curtains, switching off fans, or a microphone and see which change helps the back row most.” |
| 44–52 s | Log: saved noise and clarity timeline with labelled events, if available. | “The day log shows when noise makes speech harder to follow. It stores levels, not audio.” |
| 52–58 s | Results: Share report (PDF), then the re-measure option or an existing before-and-after card. | “Finally, I share a PDF and remeasure to check the fix.” |

The narration is about 110 words. Speak naturally; leave the final second for the screen to settle. If there is no completed log, use 44–52 seconds for the Results seat map and the Fixes voice chart, and replace the Log sentence with: “The charts also show which seats need more voice and where background noise masks speech.”

**Do not tap “Re-measure after a change” solely for the video.** It starts a new comparison and clears the current “after” seat readings. Show the option, or show a comparison that you already made in real fieldwork.

## Two-minute presentation version

This is a fuller script for a class presentation or a separate showcase video. **The assignment submission still has a one-minute maximum**; use the 58-second version above for that deliverable. Aim for a calm pace of about 120 words per minute. Use a saved room with real readings, and prepare any day log or before-and-after comparison before recording.

| Time | Show on the phone | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12 s | Open on the 3D classroom, then show the Room tab and room dimensions. | “Imagine sitting at the back of a classroom. You can hear the teacher, but the words are hard to follow. ClearRoom helps explain why.” |
| 12–27 s | Show the floor plan, seat layout, and saved room. | “I start by entering the classroom size and seating layout. The app builds a visual model of the room, so the results can be shown where students actually sit.” |
| 27–45 s | Measure tab: pick a seat, show clap echo times, noise, voice, and the live level meter if useful. Cut between completed readings instead of waiting through a recording. | “Next, the phone microphone listens to a clap to estimate how long sound lingers. It also measures background noise and the speaker’s voice. I repeat the clap at different seats, and ClearRoom checks the quality of each reading.” |
| 45–65 s | Results verdict and range; rotate the 3D room, select a back-row desk, then switch to the seat and cause maps. | “Results give each seat a clarity score from zero to one, with higher meaning clearer. The back-row verdict includes an uncertainty range. Colour on the 3D desks and seat map reveals difficult spots; the cause map explains why.” |
| 65–82 s | Scroll through the echo-by-pitch and speech-versus-distance charts; briefly show a selected seat’s detail. | “The charts make the diagnosis more specific. I can see which pitches linger in the room and how clarity changes farther from the speaker. Tapping a seat reveals the readings behind its result.” |
| 82–99 s | Fixes tab: ranked fixes, change curtains or microphone control, show the 3D change and row comparison. | “Now I can test practical ideas before spending money. For example, adding curtains, switching off fans, or using a microphone changes the predicted back-row score. The ranking and before-and-after view show which option helps most.” |
| 99–110 s | Log tab: a completed noise timeline with event labels and the lower clarity panel. | “A day log shows when background noise rises and how it may affect the back row. It stores sound levels and labels, never audio recordings.” |
| 110–120 s | Results: PDF share action, then finish on the classroom view or an existing real before-and-after comparison. | “I can share a one-page PDF, then remeasure after a real change to check whether clarity improved.” |

If you have no completed day log, use 99–110 seconds to show the required-voice chart in Fixes and say: “The voice chart shows how much louder the speaker would need to be at each row, and when shouting would still not solve the echo.” If you use the built-in sample room, identify its readings as made-up on screen and in the narration. Keep the screen recorder’s microphone **off** and add this narration afterwards.
